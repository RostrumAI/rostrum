# Claims and timelessness

Use this guidance for product documentation, reference pages, security and performance claims, comparisons, release language, and statements that may outlive a specific release.

## Timeless documentation

- Describe the current behavior directly. Do not narrate what changed in a diff.
- Avoid time anchors such as `currently`, `now`, `new`, `latest`, `soon`, `existing`, `old`, `future`, `at present`, and `as of this writing` in timeless product or reference documentation.
- Use a specific version or date when a historical or release-scoped statement is necessary.
- Put announcements, plans, and future features in release notes, changelogs, or another explicitly time-scoped document.

## Claims

- Avoid unsupported superlatives and guarantees such as `best`, `fastest`, `simplest`, `always`, `never`, `secure`, `guarantees`, and `ensures`.
- Scope performance, cost, capacity, security, and comparison claims by condition and audience.
- Cite a measurement or authoritative source when a specific number matters.
- Describe design and intended benefits rather than absolute security promises.
- Avoid disparaging comparisons with other products unless the comparison is accurate, sourced, and scoped.

## Review checklist

- [ ] The text describes current behavior rather than a change history.
- [ ] Time-sensitive language is versioned or moved to a release-oriented document.
- [ ] Claims are factual, scoped, and verifiable.
- [ ] Security language does not promise an outcome the product cannot guarantee.
- [ ] Comparisons identify their conditions and source.

Sources: [Google Timeless documentation](https://developers.google.com/style/timeless-documentation), [Documenting future features](https://developers.google.com/style/future), and [Avoid excessive claims](https://developers.google.com/style/excessive-claims).
