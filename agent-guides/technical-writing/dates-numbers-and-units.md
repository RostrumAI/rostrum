# Dates, numbers, phones, and units

Use this guidance for dates, times, quantities, measurements, phone numbers, mathematical values, and numeric examples.

## Dates and times

- Use `January 19, 2017` in prose.
- Include the weekday only when it matters: `Tuesday, April 27, 2021`.
- Use `2017-04-15` when a numeric-only or machine-readable date is required.
- Use `3 PM` or `3:45 PM`, with a space before `AM` or `PM` and no periods.
- Use a hyphen for ranges such as `5-10 minutes` in ordinary prose.
- Name the time zone and include a UTC offset only when the time zone matters.
- Do not use seasons as dates. Use a month, quarter, version, or explicit date.

## Numbers

Use the number format required by the domain, API, code, or surrounding document. Make units, decimal precision, ranges, bases, percentages, and ordering unambiguous. Do not use a number as a substitute for a missing definition or a scope statement. Cite a source when a performance, cost, capacity, or security number matters.

## Phone numbers

Use fictional numbers in examples. For US examples, use `800-555-0100` through `800-555-0199`. Do not use a real person's or organization's phone number in documentation.

## Units of measurement

- Include a unit when a quantity needs one and keep the unit format consistent within the document.
- Use the notation required by the technical domain or project convention.
- Distinguish decimal, binary, rate, duration, and storage units when the difference matters.
- Put a space between a number and a unit when the convention requires it, such as `5 minutes` or `3 PM`.
- Define an unfamiliar unit before relying on it in an instruction or comparison.

## Mathematical notation

Use the notation required by the field and explain variables, operators, constraints, and units. Format mathematical variables distinctly from ordinary prose when the document format supports it. Do not use a symbol or equation as the only explanation of a behavior or requirement.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Dates, times, ranges, and time zones are unambiguous.
- [ ] Numbers have the precision, scope, and units the reader needs.
- [ ] Example phone numbers are fictional and reserved.
- [ ] Measurements use consistent and domain-appropriate notation.
- [ ] Mathematical notation is explained in surrounding prose.
